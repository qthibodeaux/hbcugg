# Pass Defense Deep Dive: the why, the questions, the charts (2026)

**Scope:** the 18 programs, garbage time out. MEAC teams have played fewer games against the 18, so their samples are small; their unranked rows are faded.

## Why we did it

> Pass defense is more than "yards allowed." A defense can give up few yards because nobody throws on it, or look fine snap to snap and still lose games on one deep ball.
>
> So we broke it down: what opponents try, where it works, what the defense gives up, and when it matters most.

## The questions, and the chart that answers each

| Chart | The question | How the chart answers it |
|---|---|---|
| **Who shuts down the pass?** (Passing success allowed × Explosive passes allowed) | Is this defense hard to throw on, and does it give up the big one? | Each logo is a defense. Up = fewer successful throws allowed, right = fewer explosive passes. Top right shuts it down; bottom left gets thrown on. |
| **Who wins first down?** (1st down passing success allowed × Explosive passes allowed) | Does the defense set the tone on first down, or let offenses stay ahead of schedule? | The same layout, first down only. Winning first down puts offenses behind the sticks. |
| **Behind the sticks** (3rd & 7+ pass plays: first downs allowed) | When the offense needs 7+ on 3rd down, does the defense finish the stop? | One bar per defense: how often it still gives up the first down. Short and green beats The 18 average; red is worse. |
| **Red zone resistance** (Touchdowns allowed per red zone throw) | Inside the 20, does the defense keep throws out of the end zone? | The share of red-zone throws that went for six, with the raw count ("2 of 15") on every bar. |
| **Closing time** (4th QTR, 1 score game: passing success rate allowed) | With the game on the line, does the pass defense hold up? | Success allowed in the 4th quarter of one-score games. Small samples, so the counts are printed. |
| **Air or after the catch** (Yards per attempt allowed × Yards after catch allowed) | When a pass is completed, is the damage in coverage (how far the ball went) or in tackling (what happens after the catch)? | Right = the ball travels less; up = fewer yards after the catch. Bottom right = missed tackles; top left = beaten deep. |
| **Where they get hit** (Passing depth success rate) | Do opponents beat them short, over the middle distance, or deep? | Three bars per defense, short, medium and deep, each green or red against The 18 at that depth. |
| **Yards allowed to** | Who is doing the damage: receivers, tight ends or backs? | Each bar is passing yards allowed per game, split into what WRs, TEs and RBs gained. |
| **After halftime** (Passing success allowed first half vs second half) | Who adjusts at the break, and who falls apart? | Each defense's first-half rate to its second-half rate. Green moved the right way, red the wrong way. |
| **Explosive prevention** (Explosive rate) | How often does the defense give up a 20+ yard pass, and how much of its damage comes from those plays? | The rate of 20+ yard gains per throw, plus the share of all passing yards those plays made up. |
| **Ball hawks** (Pass disruption rate) | Who gets their hands on the ball? | How often a throw is broken up or intercepted. Higher is better. |

## Notes for the tweets

- **"Explosive" = a completion of 20+ yards.**
- **"Success"** = the play kept the offense on schedule: half the yards needed on 1st down, 70% on 2nd, a first down on 3rd/4th.
- **MEAC teams** have played fewer games against the 18. Their small samples are faded at the bottom and unranked.
- **The dashed line on every chart** is The 18 average.
