# SWAC Penalties deep dive: tweet copy (draft, 2026-10-27)

Every number comes from `src/data/penaltiesSwac2026W8.json`: SWAC games through Week 8, 26 games, 415 accepted flags. The posters carry the numbers; these tweets carry the explaining. Edit freely.

## Plain-language definitions (use in any tweet)

- **Expected points:** how many points a team can expect to score from a given down, distance and spot. A 1st & 10 at the opponent's 30 is worth more than a 3rd & 15 at your own 20.
- **Points given away by a flag:** how much a flag lowered the flagged team's expected points. It only counts flags that wiped out the snap, because on those the flag *is* the play.
- **Crucial flag:** a flag on 3rd or 4th down, in the red zone, on goal-to-go or in the two-minute drill, while the game was within 8 points (garbage time is left out).
- **Bounce-back:** after its own flag, the offense still picked up a first down or scored on that drive.

## 1 · The flag battle

> Who wins the flag battle in the SWAC?
>
> Net penalty yards = the yards a team's opponents are flagged for, minus its own.
>
> Southern: +20.6 a game, the best in the SWAC.
> MVSU: −26.2 a game, with 123.5 penalty yards of its own every game.
>
> Fewer flags won only 13 of 25 games. Counting flags doesn't pick winners.

## 2 · What flags cost

> Not every flag hurts the same.
>
> We measured each flag by the points it gave away: how much worse the down, distance and spot became for the team that drew it.
>
> MVSU's flags cost it 9.7 points a game. Southern's cost 2.9.
>
> Crucial flags are the ones on 3rd and 4th down, in the red zone or the last two minutes of a close game. 44% of UAPB's flags are crucial, the highest share in the SWAC.

**Optional follow-up (foul by foul):**

> By foul:
> Pass interference gave away 81 points and 41 automatic first downs, the most of any foul.
> Holding: the most called (91). It erased 12 first downs that had already been gained.
> Roughing the passer: 1.8 points a flag, the most expensive flag to throw.

**Biggest single flag:** JSU pass interference at Grambling with 2:00 left cut JSU's chance to win by 24 points. Grambling won 45–38.

## 3 · After the flag

> A flag doesn't end a drive. Who bounces back?
>
> Each square on the poster is one offensive flag. A filled square means the offense still moved the chains or scored on that drive.
>
> AAMU recovered after 10 of its 12 offensive flags. ASU recovered after 1 of 7.
>
> MVSU's offense had 8 flags on 3rd down and kept the series alive once. Alcorn did it 3 times in 4, and all 3 drives scored.

## 4 · Habits

> The same foul every week is a habit, not bad luck.
>
> TSU cut its pre-snap flags from 16 to 5. Those are the fouls a team controls entirely.
> MVSU: 10, 15, 14, 15 flags a game.
> AAMU's pre-snap flags doubled (3 → 6).
>
> In every game they've played: false starts for TSU, Grambling and Alcorn; holding for PVAMU, MVSU, UAPB and B-CU; pass interference for AAMU and B-CU.
>
> Flags are drifting down league-wide.
