# MEAC/SWAC Challenge Design Doc

## Overview

The **MEAC/SWAC Challenge** is the annual Week 0 curtain-raiser for HBCU football. Owned by ESPN Events and televised nationally on ESPN, it serves as the inter-conference bridge that starts the season (matching the Celebration Bowl which ends it).

**Current Venue:** Center Parc Stadium (Atlanta, GA)
**Current Date:** Week 0 (Saturday)
**Series Standing:** MEAC 11, SWAC 7 (1 No Contest, 1 Covid Cancellation)

## Conference Founding & Early History

### SWAC Founding (1920)

- **Date:** September 10, 1920, Houston, Texas
- **Original "Super Six" Charter Members:**
  - Bishop College (Marshall, TX) — 1920 to 1956
  - Paul Quinn College (Waco, TX) — 1920 to 1929
  - Prairie View State Normal & Industrial College, now Prairie View A&M (Prairie View, TX) — 1920 to Present (only remaining charter member)
  - Samuel Huston College, now Huston-Tillotson University (Austin, TX) — 1920 to 1954
  - Texas College (Tyler, TX) — 1920 to 1962
  - Wiley College (Marshall, TX) — 1920 to 1968
- **First Football Championship:** 1921, Wiley College (inaugural title)
- **Key Early Expansions:**
  - Langston University — 1931 to 1957
  - Southern University — 1934 (still member)
  - Arkansas AM&N (now Arkansas-Pine Bluff) — 1936 to 1970, rejoined 1997
  - Texas Southern University — 1954 (still member)
  - Grambling College (now Grambling State University) — 1958 (still member)
  - Jackson College for Negro Teachers (now Jackson State University) — 1958 (still member)

### MEAC Founding (1970)

- **Date:** 1970 confirmation, first football season 1971
- **Formation Meeting:** 1969, Durham, North Carolina
- **Seven Charter Members:**
  - Delaware State University
  - Howard University
  - University of Maryland Eastern Shore
  - Morgan State University
  - North Carolina A&T State University
  - North Carolina Central University
  - South Carolina State College (now University)
- **Background:** Breakaway from CIAA for Division I aspirations
- **Division I Status:** Achieved June 8, 1978 (previously Division II)
- **First Expansion (1979):** Bethune-Cookman College and Florida A&M University joined

## Conference Realignment Timeline

### Major SWAC Changes

- 1962: Alcorn A&M (now Alcorn State University) joined
- 1968: Mississippi Valley State University joined
- 1982: Alabama State University joined
- 1999: Alabama A&M University joined
- 2021: Florida A&M University and Bethune-Cookman University joined (MEAC departure)
- 2021: Divisional realignment - FAMU/BCU to East, Alcorn State to West

### Major MEAC Changes

- 1979: Bethune-Cookman and Florida A&M join; Morgan State, North Carolina Central, and Maryland Eastern Shore withdraw
- 1981: Maryland Eastern Shore rejoins
- 1984: Morgan State rejoins; Florida A&M leaves (returns 1986)
- 1985: Coppin State College joins
- 1995: Hampton University joins
- 1997: Norfolk State University joins
- 2010: North Carolina Central rejoins
- 2018: Hampton leaves for Big South
- 2019: Savannah State departs for Division II
- 2021: North Carolina A&T leaves for Big South (founding member departure)
- 2021: Florida A&M and Bethune-Cookman leave for SWAC

## Bowl Game Chronologies

### Pelican Bowl (1972-1975) - Black College National Championship

- **1972:** Grambling 56, North Carolina Central 6 (Wallace Wade Stadium, Durham, NC - 22,500)
- **1973:** Canceled due to campus unrest at Grambling
- **1974:** Grambling 28, South Carolina State 7 (Tulane Stadium, New Orleans, LA - 30,120)
- **1975:** Southern 20, South Carolina State 7 (Memorial Stadium, Jackson, MS)
- **Context:** NCAA Division II era, attendance issues, no TV deals led to collapse

### Gold Bowl Era (1976-1980)

- MEAC vs CIAA matchup series
- Limited documentation available
- Transition period as MEAC moved toward Division I

### Heritage Bowl (1991-1999) - MEAC vs SWAC Championship

- **1991:** Alabama State 35, North Carolina A&T 28 (Joe Robbie Stadium, Miami Gardens, FL - 7,728)
- **1992:** Grambling State 52, Florida A&M 35 (Bragg Memorial Stadium, Tallahassee, FL - 11,273)
- **1993:** Southern 31, South Carolina State 27 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **1994:** Grambling State 41, South Carolina State 6 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA) - First true champion vs champion matchup
- **1995:** Southern 27, Florida A&M 14 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **1996:** Southern 30, Howard 25 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **1997:** Southern 28, South Carolina State 7 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **1998:** Southern 34, Bethune-Cookman 27 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **1999:** Southern 24, Hampton 3 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA)
- **Failure Reasons:** Conference champions declining for NCAA playoffs, sponsor issues, uneven participation

### Celebration Bowl (2015-Present) - MEAC vs SWAC Championship

- **2015:** North Carolina A&T 41, Grambling State 34 (Georgia Dome, Atlanta, GA - 35,642)
- **2016:** Grambling State 27, North Carolina A&T 20 (Georgia Dome - 22,771)
- **2017:** North Carolina A&T 21, Grambling State 14 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 35,321)
- **2018:** North Carolina A&T 24, Alcorn State 22 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 42,212)
- **2019:** North Carolina A&T 64, Alcorn State 44 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 50,319)
- **2020:** Canceled (COVID-19)
- **2021:** South Carolina State 31, Jackson State 10 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 40,630)
- **2022:** North Carolina Central 21, Jackson State 14 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 49,721)
- **2023:** Florida A&M 31, Howard 27 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 47,892)
- **2024:** South Carolina State 34, Jackson State 31 (Mercedes-Benz Stadium - 51,324)

## Championship Structures

### SWAC Football Championship Game

- **Started:** 1999
- **Format:** East vs West division champions
- **Venue:** Legion Field, Birmingham, AL (historically)
- **Context:** Created after SWAC split into divisions for football

### MEAC Football Championship

- **Format:** Regular season champion (no championship game)
- **NCAA Playoff Participation:** MEAC champions frequently participated in I-AA/FCS playoffs instead of bowl games

- **College Division Era:** Early Pelican Bowl years
